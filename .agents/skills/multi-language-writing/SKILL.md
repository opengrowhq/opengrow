# Multi-Language Writing

## Grounding status

**Methodology-only.** OpenGrow has no locale field or multi-language content pipeline yet. This skill applies once such a feature exists.

## Overview

Locale-aware writing is not translation — it adapts examples, idioms, currency/units, cultural references, and search intent phrasing to how a specific locale's audience actually thinks and searches, not just their language.

## When to Use

- Content is being written or adapted for a specific target locale (not just language)

## When NOT to Use

- Straight literal translation is explicitly what's wanted (different task, different skill)

## Core Pattern

1. **Localize examples**, not just words — currency, units of measurement, date formats, region-specific references (laws, holidays, well-known brands).
2. **Match local search phrasing**, not a literal translation of the English keyword — how a query is actually typed in that locale can differ structurally from a direct translation.
3. **Respect cultural tone norms** — formality level, directness, and humor conventions vary by locale even at equivalent reading levels.
4. **Verify facts are still accurate for that locale** — pricing, availability, legal claims may not transfer as-is.

## Checklist

- [ ] Currency/units/dates localized, not left in source-locale format
- [ ] Examples and references make sense to the target locale's audience
- [ ] Keyword/heading phrasing matches how the locale actually searches, not a literal translation
- [ ] Locale-specific facts (pricing, legal, availability) verified, not assumed to carry over

## Common Mistakes

- Shipping a literal translation and calling it localized.
- Keeping source-locale currency/date formats.
- Assuming search intent phrasing translates 1:1 across languages.
